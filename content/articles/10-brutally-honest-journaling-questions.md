If you ever came in contact with journaling, you probably thought it was like writing a diary:

“Dear diary, today I did X and then Y…”

That might feel nice in the moment, but it rarely changes anything.

One of the greatest strengths of the human mind is its ability to ask itself better questions. The right questions act like lenses: they reframe your situation, reveal blind spots, and turn vague discomfort into clear insight.

Tony Robbins put it well: *“The quality of your life is determined by the quality of the questions you ask yourself.”*

If you ask yourself the right questions, answer them honestly, and then act on those answers, it becomes almost impossible **not** to make progress in the next weeks and months.

And no, I’m not talking about shallow questions like “How can I be better?” that barely scratch the surface. I’m talking about questions that make you uncomfortable. Questions that force you to re-evaluate the way you’re living, working, eating, training, and spending your time.

That is what real journaling is about:
**Using deep questions to rethink your assumptions and see your life from a new angle.**

Science is starting to catch up with this idea. Regular journaling has been shown to reduce stress and anxiety, improve mood, and help you organize your thoughts and patterns more clearly, exactly what you need if you want to stop running your life on autopilot.

## How to use this article in the right way

Pick 3-5 questions from the list each day and answer everyone in 2-5 honest lines, without striving for the perfect answer.

End the journaling session with one concrete action you’ll take based on your answers.

Now let’s get right into the questions!

---

## Question 1: What am I avoiding right now and why?

What you avoid is most of the time exactly the thing where the next level of your life is hiding.

Hard conversations with a loved one, scary emails or important decisions, these things run your life silently in the background when you do not face them. They drain your self-esteem and gratitude while increasing stress, because your brain knows you’re running away.
When you give that damn “thing” a name and write down why you’re avoiding it (fear of failure, conflict, embarrassment), you take it out of the dark and into the light. Once you can clearly see it, it becomes less scary and much more actionable.

### But how can you really get the most out of this question?

**Step 1: List one thing you want to avoid today. Be as specific as possible (for example: “Doing 5 cold outreach calls”)**

**Step 2: Write one honest sentence that starts with: “I’m avoiding this because...”**

**Step 3: Write the smallest next step you could take in under 10 minutes**

**Step 4: Do the step today, without hesitation**

You’re training yourself to stop negotiating with fear and start moving through it.

## Question 2: “What is the single most important result I must achieve today?”

Most of the days do not fail because you were “lazy”, but because you did everything except the thing that truly mattered the most.
You change your profile picture, tweak the bio, answer all e-mails and stay up to date on every notification but the really big task, like cold calling 5 businesses or producing that video, gets postponed until “tomorrow.”
And let’s be real, the same cycle starts tomorrow morning again...

This question forces you to pick the one **must-win outcome** of the day. This is not a to-do list, but the one task that if it is not finished, you can’t go to sleep.
The task should be very specific, not “write some emails”, but “send 3 pitches to local businesses advertising my new service”, not “work on youtube”, but “writing the script for a new 10 minute video about the importance of journaling in your day to day life”

### How can you get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Ask yourself: “If I could only get one thing done today, what would move my life or business forward the most”**

**Step 2: Write the task as a clear, measurable result (for example: “Book 1 sales call” or “Outreach to 5 potential clients per phone”)**

**Step 3: Block 60-90 minutes in your day for the one task, ideally as early as possible (eat the frog first)**

**Step 4: End your day by writing: “Did I conquer my must-do?” and be brutally honest with yourself**

Over time, your brain learns that your day is judged by outcomes, not just by how busy you felt.

## Question 3: “What am I grateful for that I did not have a year ago”

This question counteracts the high-achiever trap: you always see what you want to achieve, but you rarely see what you have already achieved. That mindset quietly drains your whole motivation and well-being, because you will tell yourself, that you will be happy once you achieved the next goal. And then the next goal comes and you already have the next goal in mind, without feeling the pleasure.

By anchoring gratitude in time, you force yourself to notice progress instead of only noticing what’s missing.

**“What do I have now that my past self would have killed for?”.**
Let’s say you want to lose 50 pounds and already have lost 30 pounds. Instead of solely focussing on the 20 pounds you have to lose extra, let’s just focus one moment on the 30 pounds you have already lost! That’s damn amazing.

### How can you get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Write the date from one year ago at the top of the page**

**Step 2: List 3 things you have now that you did not have then: skills, relationships, habits, money, health, opportunities,...**

**Step 3: For each one, finish the following sentence: “This proves that...”**

- “...I can lose weight if I stay consistent with my diet and my workouts”
- “...I can actually stick to a training plan”
- “...I can learn new skills and build a business while working full-time”

With this question, you are training your brain to see evidence that you can change over time, which makes future change feel more realistic instead of impossible.

## Question 4: “What did I learn yesterday that I do not want to forget”

If you do not capture your lessons, life becomes a loop of repeated mistakes and forgotten insights.
And like Einstein said *“Insanity is doing the same thing over and over again and expecting different results”*
So this question turns every day into a class and every experience into data you can reuse in the future.
It might be a gym lesson like “heavy deadlifts in the evening ruin my sleep”, a social insight “when I ask more questions, conversations feel easier” or a business lesson “cold DMs work better when I lead with value, not my bio”.
A nice extra is, that when you write it down, you automatically engrain the lesson into your brain and it will sustain longer in your memory.

### How can you get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Recall one moment from yesterday that stands out, like a win, a mistake, an emotion or a deep conversation**

**Step 2: Answer “What did this teach me?” in one sentence.**

**Step 3: Write down how you will use this insight today and give yourself a small application**

Over the months and years to come, you will build a personal operating manual, which is written from your own life experiences rather than generic advice from books, videos or podcasts.

## Question 5: “What is one thing I can do today that my future self will genuinely thank me for?”

Think of your future self as a real person you either help or sabotage every day.

Whether you are eating a KFC Burger or ignoring your budget and spending way too much money on the new iPhone, you will give your future self a few extra problems it has to deal with.
On the other hand, whether you are avoiding the junk food for just a protein shake or go to the gym although you do not feel like it or finally start to invest, you will give your future self a healthier, calmer and richer life.
It is basically the **easy-hard paradox:**

Choose the easy options now (like junk food or playing video games) and life gets hard later, or choose hard now (like a salad and skipping video games for an extra hour of cold calling) and life gets easy later.
When you ask what your future self would genuinely thank you for, you naturally choose behaviors that will compound over time like training, learning, creating, investing or sleeping well.

### How can you get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Imagine yourself in 1 - 5 years ahead: What is he doing? How does he look, feel and live?**

**Step 2: Ask yourself: “What tiny action today would make his life easier?”**

- Cooking a healthy meal
- Reading 10 pages of a self-help book
- Sending one important mail

**Step 3: Commit to only one activity and write: “Today, I will do X for future me.”**

Then act like it is a promise to someone you respect, because it genuinely is!

## Question 6: “What small act of courage can I take today?”

Your comfort zone does not shrink overnight, it shrinks every time you choose the easy, comfortable path.

Courage works the same way, but also in the other way: tiny, courageous actions repeated daily will make you a person who does hard things by default. Courage is like a muscle, that is trained every time you use it and will grow over time.

Every time you take a small step like posting a video, cold calling a potential client or smile at a stranger in public, you gradually make bigger moves feel normal. This will stretch your comfort zone to a new level. And your life will only grow as much as your comfort zone does.
And this question keeps you living on the edge of your comfort zone.

### How can you get the most out of this question?

**Step 1: Identify 1 area where fear is quietly in charge: relationships, content, business, fitness, etc**

**Step 2: Write 1 small, scary but safe action you can take today:**

- Post your honest opinion
- Pitch to one potential client
- Start a conversation with a random person on the street

**Step 3: Rate it on a scale from 1-10 on a “fear scale”. You should aim at least for a solid 4-6, which will feel uncomfortable, but not paralyzing**

**Step 4: Do it within 24 hours and write down how it actually felt versus how you expected it to feel.**

You’ll notice it almost always felt less scary than your brain predicted. That realization alone can change your behavior for life.

## Question 7: “If I could only work for 2 hours this week, what would I work on?”

This, just like question 2, will help you brutally focus on the most important tasks in your work day.

Imagine someone forces you to only work 2 hours this week. What would you do in those 120 minutes?
When you pretend you only have two hours, all the fake productivity disappears. You’re left with the very few actions that create almost all your progress.
Thinking this way is basically applying the **Pareto principle** (20% of efforts give 80% of results) to your work week.

### How can you get the most out of this question?

**Step 1: List everything you think you need to do this week.**

**Step 2: Then ask yourself: “If I had only 2 hours to move my business forward, what would I do in this period?”**

**Step 3: Circle the 1-3 actions that apply to this question. Those should be your real priorities**

**Step 4: Put those in your calendar first and adjust the other tasks around them to prioritize more effectively**

## Question 8: “What do I want my legacy to be?”

Zooming out to the very end of your life cuts through a lot of noise.

This mindset will help you with the big image of your life, your purpose, your most important relationships. Legacy is not just about the “big achievements”, but also what people feel when they think of you and what your life stands for.
Just imagine at the end of your life, you will see your **life as a movie**. Every day was filmed. And there are thousands of people in the cinema. Would your life be just like a repeated cycle of spreadsheet work, junk-food consumption and all-nighters in Fortnite?

Or will it be a movie about the journey of an underdog rising against all the odds to the top of his league. Will the viewers get bored and leave the movie after 10 minutes?

And after you’re gone, **what will your children and grandchildren say** when someone asks them about you?

If your current habits don’t match the legacy you say you want, that discomfort is a powerful source of motivation.

### How can you get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Write a short paragraph as if someone is giving a speech about you at your 90th birthday or funeral.**

- What did you build?
- How did you treat people?
- What did you stand for?

**Step 2: Underline 3 key words or themes (for example “discipline”, “kindness”, “courageous”)**

**Step 3: Ask yourself: “What is one way my day today reflects these words, or contradicts them?”**

**Step 4: Choose one small behavior that would bring your day 1% closer to that legacy**

With this question in mind, you will turn “someday I will be that person” into “today I will act like that person”

## Question 9: “If I had all the resources in the world, how would I spend my day”

Money excuses are a powerful tool to procrastinate on life: “I would do X, but I do not have the money for it”
This question temporarily removes that barrier and **reveals what you actually want out of life**. How would you invest your time if survival was not on the table?

Would you still go to your job because you love it that much or would you rather travel the world with your children? Would you build something new?

No one is saying you should quit your job tomorrow, leave your wife and kids and become a monk, but knowing the real answer tells you what direction your life should gradually move towards. And maybe your dream life is not as far away as you think it is.

### How can you get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Pretend all your living expenses are covered forever. Seriously. Rent, food, everything!**

**Step 2: Write a detailed “day in the life” for that version of you:**

- When do you wake up?
- What work do you do (if any)?
- Who are you around?
- How do you move your body?

**Step 3: Highlight 2-3 elements you could partially integrate now:**

- If you’d “create every day” can you create 30 minutes a day now?
- If you’d “train like an athlete”, can you train 4 times a week now?

**Step 4: Pick one and implement it this week**

You don’t need infinite money to start living like the person you imagine, you just need to steal small pieces from that future and bring them into the present.

## Question 10: “How is my story holding me back?”

**Your actions follow your story.**

If you keep telling yourself, “I’m just not social,” “I’m lazy,” or “I always quit,” your brain will work hard to keep that story consistent, even when it’s the opposite of what you really want.

Rewriting your story doesn’t mean lying to yourself, it means deliberately collecting and focusing on evidence that supports a more empowering story.
Let’s say you are an introvert, but you want to be more extroverted. The default operating system of your mind will work against you, because your current story is that you are an introvert since you were born. Here is the crucial part that you work to change this story through small actions, that prove this story wrong. Every time you talk to a new person, you are proving that this story is wrong!

### How to get the most out of this question:

**Step 1: Write down 1 limiting story you tell yourself, exactly as it sounds in your head**

- “I am bad with new people”
- “I can not stick to routines because in second class, my art teacher told me so”
- “I am not the entrepreneurial type”

**Step 2: Ask yourself where you learned this and if it is 100% true.**

**Step 3: List 3 memories (even the tiny ones) that contradict this story**

- A time where you *were* social
- A habit you *did* stick to
- An entrepreneurial risk you *did* take

**Step 4: Rewrite your story in one new sentence**

- “I was the kid that was shy around new people but I am learning and improving, and I already have proof”
- “I can stick to routines when I have designed them in a way that feels like a video game”

After these steps, live every day as if the new sentence were true. That is how you slowly become someone else.
I was a kid that had the story of “I am addicted to sugar, especially chocolate” and was overweight for a couple of years. But one time (when my mother called me fat), I changed my story instantly and in my mind, I became an athlete. That helped me to go from 260 pounds down to currently 180 pounds.

## Summary

If you want to get the most out of this article, use this as a simple daily practice:

**Step 1: Each morning or evening, pick 3 questions from this or another list (I will continue with those lists if it helped you!)**

**Step 2: Answer them honestly in a physical notebook or notes app**

**Step 3: End every session with one line: “Because of what I wrote down today, I will do _ tomorrow/today”**

The goal of journaling is not to write beautiful pages.
The goal is to ask better questions, tell yourself the truth, and then act on that truth.

Your life changes when your standards change, and these questions are a powerful place to start.
